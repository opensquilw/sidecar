# 參考資料（繁體中文）

## 交通意外
冇人會預咗會撞車，所以頭幾分鐘一定係亂嘅。照下面個次序一步步做就得。呢度係香港嘅一般指引 —— 警察、保險公司同運輸署先係最終權威。

### 即刻：停車，先確保安全
- 停車。唔好走: 喺香港，意外後不停車係刑事罪行 —— 就算睇落好輕微、就算你覺得唔係你錯都一樣。
- 開死火燈: 安全嘅話，喺車後遠啲放三角警告牌 —— 快速公路要放得比街道遠好多。
- 人先離開危險位: 喺快速公路，全部人落車企去欄杆外面。停喺行車線入面嘅車，係全世界最危險嘅位置。
- 有人受傷？即刻打 999: 除非傷者有即時危險（起火、車衝埋嚟），否則唔好郁佢。講清楚你喺邊、幾多人、你見到咩。

### 重撞之後嘅第一分鐘
- 未郁之前，先檢查自己: 腎上腺素會遮住痛楚。郁下手指腳趾、感受下頸痛唔痛、望下有冇血。頸或者背痛嘅話，除非架車着火，否則唔好郁，等救護車。
- 安全氣袋嘅煙係正常: 氣袋彈出之後嘅白粉同燶味，係推進劑，唔係起火。但黑煙、火、或者好重汽油味就唔同 —— 全部人落車，離開最少 30 米，唔好開車頭冚。
- 熄匙，開死火燈: 掂到匙就熄咗佢。熄匙會停油泵，減低起火風險。
- 被困？唔好硬撐: 打 999，講明你被困，同佢哋保持通話。除非有火，否則唔好畀旁人拉你出嚟 —— 脊椎受傷被人拖出嚟，比等一等傷得更重。
- 坐緊安全座椅嘅小朋友: 除非有即時危險，否則唔好解開。如果佢話頸痛背痛、或者好眼瞓，等救護員嚟先郁佢。
- 跟住先做基本嘢: 所有人穩定咗之後：由背向行車線嗰邊落車、企去欄杆外面、放三角牌。之後就跟下面「任何意外」嗰啲步驟。

### 有人受傷
- 先打 999，再幫手: 講位置、幾多人傷、有冇人失去知覺或者被困。開免提，空出雙手。
- 懷疑頸或者背受傷，唔好郁佢: 除非有火或者車流令留喺原位更危險。做得到嘅話，扶穩佢個頭唔好郁。
- 流血：用力㩒實，一直㩒: 乾淨嘅布、件 T 恤，乜都得。唔好揭開嚟望。做得到就抬高嗰隻手腳。
- 失去知覺但有呼吸: 識急救就用復原臥式。唔識就保持氣道暢通，等。留喺佢身邊。
- 保暖，同佢講嘢: 休克嚟得好快。一件褸，一把冷靜嘅聲。唔好畀嘢食畀水飲。
- 唔好走: 就算你覺得自己冇事、對方態度好差都一樣。等警察話可以走先走 —— 留低傷者離開係好嚴重嘅罪行。

### 對方走咗（被撞逃逸）
- 唔好追: 記低車牌、顏色、車廠、方向、時間。記到一半車牌都有用。寫唔切就對住手機語音備忘嗌出嚟。
- 即刻儲低車 Cam 片: 大部分車 Cam 幾個鐘之內就會循環覆蓋。做任何嘢之前，先拔記憶卡或者鎖定條片。
- 目擊者同鏡頭: 問附近嘅人攞姓名電話。記低附近有冇舖頭、大廈、巴士可能有鏡頭 —— 警察可以攞片，但要知道去邊度攞。
- 即刻報警: 有人傷打 999；否則 24 小時內去警署或者打報案熱線。任何索償都要報案編號。
- 如果永遠搵唔到對方: 全保會賠你架車。至於被無牌／搵唔到嘅司機撞傷，汽車保險局（Motor Insurers' Bureau）有賠償計劃 —— 問你保險公司或者律師。

### 嚴重撞車之後嗰幾日
- 覺得冇事都要去檢查: 鞭索式頸傷、腦震盪、內出血好多時 24–72 小時之後先浮現。當日就去急症或者睇醫生，講明係撞車，所有收據同報告留低 —— 冇一開始嘅醫療記錄，人身傷害索償係做唔到嘅。
- 驚魂未定就唔好揸車: 腎上腺素會突然退。搵人接你，或者搭的士。呢個唔係軟弱，係避開第二次意外。
- 口供: 畀警察一份照實嘅口供。未同自己保險公司傾過之前，唔好畀對方保險公司或者理賠員錄口供 —— 你冇義務要畀。
- 架車全損（write-off）: 保險公司賠嘅係市值，唔係你買嘅價錢。你可以用證據爭（近期估價、保養記錄、低里數）。未供完嘅車貸照樣要還 —— 睇下張保單有冇差額保障。
- 人身傷害索償: 向有錯一方嘅保險公司索償。記一本簡單日記：痛楚、請假、開支、收據。香港人身傷害索償嘅時限一般係以年計，唔係以月計 —— 但證據唔會等你，早啲開始。
- 收到傳票: 好多嚴重撞車之後都會有不小心駕駛控罪，就算你覺得冇人有錯。唔好未問過意見就郵寄認罪。當值律師服務有免費法律諮詢計劃；你保險公司亦可能提供律師。
- 照顧下自己個腦: 不斷回想、驚上路、瞓唔着，都係正常。如果幾個星期之後仲係咁，都值得同醫生講。

### 係咪一定要報警？
- 要 —— 只要有人受傷: 一定要。警察會到場，會發報案編號（RN）。之後保險索償一定要用呢個編號。
- 要 —— 對方走咗、唔合作、或者你撞到冇人喺度嘅泊車: 如果喺現場交換唔到資料，24 小時內一定要去警署報案。撞到泊住嘅車然後走人，會當你不停車處理。
- 要 —— 涉及政府車、的士、巴士、小巴: 呢啲幾乎一定會報。交畀警察處理就得。
- 可以唔報 —— 輕微損毀、冇人傷、雙方都合作: 交換晒資料就可以走。但就算咁，好多保險公司都想有警方報告 —— 睇返你張保單。唔肯定就報。

### 同對方司機交換啲咩
- 車牌號碼
- 司機全名同身份證／駕駛執照號碼
- 聯絡電話
- 保險公司同保單號碼
- 如果司機唔係車主：登記車主姓名

### 未郁之前，先影相
- 兩架車停咗嘅原位 —— 幾個角度，再加遠鏡影到成條路嘅佈局
- 兩架車每一個損毀位，近鏡
- 兩個車牌，要影到清楚睇到
- 路面：線、路牌、交通燈、煞車痕、地面、天氣
- 對方嘅駕駛執照同保險卡，如果佢肯畀你影
- 目擊者 —— 佢走之前攞低姓名同電話

### 唔好做
- 唔好認錯: 對住對方唔好、電話唔好、社交媒體都唔好。照實講發生咗咩就得。邊個有錯，係之後由保險公司或者法庭決定。
- 唔好喺路邊私下畀現金講數: 私了可以令你張保單失效，而且好多損毀係之後先發現。交畀保險公司。
- 唔好簽對方任何文件: 除咗警方嘅正式表格。
- 有傷者仲喺現場，唔好走: 等警察話你可以走先走。

### 之後 24–48 小時
- 通知保險公司: 多數保單要求 24–48 小時內、或者「盡快」通知。打保險卡上面嘅熱線，有 RN 就一併畀佢。
- 用保險公司指定嘅拖車: 架車行唔到嘅話，打保險公司嘅 24 小時拖車熱線，唔好用突然出現嘅拖車。意外現場嘅「主動」拖車係已知嘅陷阱。
- 決定點樣修: 淨係買咗第三者保險：你要向對方保險公司索償（或者自己畀）。綜合保（全保）：自己保險公司先修，再向對方追討。授權維修之前先問清楚。
- 全部嘢留低: 相、RN、對方資料、收據、拖車單。順便喺呢個 App 嘅記錄度記一筆，留低日期同里數。

### 唔係撞車 —— 係架車死咗火
- 開死火燈，安全咁盡量靠左泊 —— 快速公路就泊入路肩。
- 喺快速公路：全部人落車企欄杆外面，三角牌放遠啲。唔好喺行車線度試圖整嘢。
- 打保險公司嘅路邊援助／拖車熱線。好多全保都包。
- 喺繁忙路段爆軚，好多時拖走好過即場換。

### 車上放定呢啲
- 三角警告牌同反光背心
- 電筒（手機電筒都得，但真電筒襟用啲）
- 保險卡影印本，同埋手機上面呢個 App
- 基本急救包同手機充電線
- 筆同紙 —— 手機永遠喺最緊要嗰陣冇電

### 電話
- 999: 警察／救護／消防 —— 任何緊急情況
- 你保險公司嘅熱線: 喺保險卡上面。而家即刻存入手機。
- 1823: 政府熱線，包括運輸署查詢

## 交通守則
新手最常中招嘅規則。呢度係提示，唔係法例 —— 運輸署《道路使用者守則》先係權威，而且下面嘅數字會不時改。

### 基本
- 靠左行駛: 右邊超車。多線道路，唔係超車就靠左。
- 冇路牌就係 50 km/h: 香港所有冇標示限速嘅路，預設就係 50。快速公路會標 70、80、100 或者 110。
- 全車人都要扣安全帶: 前排後排、每個位、每次揸。乘客冇扣，司機負責。
- 唔可以手持電話: 淨係可以用固定支架免提。停紅燈拎住電話一樣算。
- 酒精上限：每 100 毫升血液 50 毫克: 對大部分人嚟講大概係一杯 —— 而且隨機抽查好常見。最安全嘅數字係零。
- 日落到日出要開車頭燈: 隧道、大雨、能見度低都要。好多車唔會自動開。

### 路口同線
- 黃格: 出口未清就唔好入。唯一例外係等右轉，而只係對頭車擋住你。
- 斑馬線: 行人一踏上去就有優先權。埋近要慢，斑馬線附近永遠唔好超車。
- 黃燈即係停: 除非停會唔安全。黃燈唔係「加速」嘅意思。
- 迴旋處: 讓已經喺迴旋處入面、由你右邊嚟嘅車。出口前打左燈。
- 實白線: 唔可以越線或者轉線 —— 包括隧道入面，成條隧道都係實線。
- 巴士線: 路牌寫住嘅時段唔可以入。有鏡頭執法。

### 停車同泊車
- 雙黃線: 任何時間都唔可以泊。單黃線：路牌寫住嘅時段唔可以泊。
- 巴士站、斑馬線、路口附近永遠唔好停: 喺巴士站「停一陣啫」係全港最多告票嘅罪行之一。
- 咪錶泊車: 預計泊幾耐就入幾耐。過鐘係定額罰款。
- 泊車唔熄匙: 泊住車開住引擎係違例，只有指定例外。熄咗佢。

### P 牌（暫准駕駛執照）—— 頭 12 個月
- 要掛 P 牌: 車頭車尾都要，每次揸車都要。
- 最高 70 km/h: 無論路牌限速幾多。
- 三線或以上嘅快速公路，唔可以行最右線: 最右線（快線）你唔可以用。
- 扣少啲分就會停牌: 暫准司機比正式牌嘅司機少啲分就會被取消資格，而且暫准期可以延長。呢一年小心啲。

### 違例記分制（DOPS）
- 兩年內 15 分 = 停牌: 記分由違例當日計起，喺你記錄度留兩年。
- 超速: 超過 15 km/h：3 分。超過 30：5 分。超過 45：10 分 —— 最嚴重嗰級仲會出傳票。
- 衝紅燈: 5 分。
- 不小心駕駛: 5 分。危險駕駛：10 分，多數仲要上庭。
- 不遵守交通標誌或者道路標記: 3 分。
- 電話、安全帶、泊車: 定額罰款。金額同係咪扣分會改 —— 睇返運輸署最新嘅表。

### 令新手唔會出事嘅習慣
- 兩秒距離: 揀一個固定物件；前車經過嗰陣開始數「一千零一、一千零二」。你未數完就經過咗，即係太貼。落雨就加倍。
- 鏡、燈、盲點、先郁: 每次轉線，每一次。香港嘅小巴同電單車就係住喺你盲點入面。
- 當所有的士同小巴都會無啦啦停: 因為佢真係會。
- 入彎前慢，唔係入咗彎先慢: 尤其係濕嘅油漆路面標記 —— 好跣。
- 斜路起步用手掣: 香港好斜。喺空嘅停車場練斜路起步，唔好喺花園道先學。
《道路使用者守則》由運輸署免費出版，係最權威嘅參考。將 PDF 存喺手機度。

## 機油：揀油步驟
- 打開車主手冊（或者睇機油蓋）: 搵兩樣嘢：黏度標號（例如 5W-30）同規格（例如 API SP、ACEA C3、VW 504.00）。兩樣都入落「我的車」。
- 標號鎖死: 冇得傾。除非有針對你部引擎嘅特定理由，否則唔好畀人游說你轉標號。
- 規格要啱或者更高: API 標準向下兼容 —— 部車要 SN，用 SP 冇問題。但車廠認證冇得彈：寫住 VW 504.00 就一定要 VW 504.00。
- 揀油種: 手冊寫住長周期、或者部車有渦輪，即係要全合成。否則舊車用半合成都算合理折衷。
- 最後先揀牌子: 去到呢步先係睇價錢、買唔買到、同個人喜好。任何一隻大牌子，只要有你要嘅標號＋規格，都做到嘢。
- 叫師傅畀返舊件你睇: 想車房老實啲，有個好簡單方法：叫佢畀你睇放出嚟嘅舊油同舊機油隔。跟住即刻喺 CarMate 記低日期、里數、牌子同標號。

## 睇懂標號：5W-30 係咩意思
5W — 「W」係 Winter（冬天）。呢個數字講緊機油喺凍嘅時候仲有幾易流動。數字越細＝凍機時越稀＝著車一開始就快啲流到引擎頂部。引擎大部分磨蝕就係喺頭嗰幾秒發生。
30 — 機油喺正常工作溫度（100°C）之下仲有幾稠。數字越大＝油膜越厚。
香港冇真正嘅冬天，所以 W 嗰個數字冇寒帶國家咁緊要。但唔代表可以亂改：新引擎油道好幼，可變汽門正時（VVT）係跟住一個指定黏度設計嘅。太稠＝機油遲到；太稀＝油膜頂唔住。跟車主手冊寫嗰個就最穩陣。
- 0W-16 / 0W-20: 新款日本引擎、油電混能、小排氣量渦輪。慳油專用標號，車廠有寫先好用。
- 5W-30: 全世界最常見。大部分近十年嘅日系同歐系汽油車。
- 5W-40: 歐洲渦輪汽油／柴油、舊款性能車、成日跑高速或者車頭好熱嗰啲。
- 10W-40: 舊車或者行咗好多公里嘅引擎，多數配半合成。十年以上嘅車好常見。
- 15W-40: 舊柴油車、貨 Van。私家車而家好少會啱。
- 0W-40: 高性能歐洲引擎（Porsche A40、部分 AMG）。淨係用高級全合成。
喺邊度搵到你部車嘅標號：車主手冊、引擎上面個入油蓋（機油蓋）、或者車頭冚入面貼紙。搵到之後入落「我的車」，第日入車房就唔使再撳手機搵。

## 三種機油
- 礦物油 (最平; 約 5,000 公里 或 3–4 個月): 直接由原油提煉，分子大細唔均勻，一熱就好快變稀，燒完仲會留多啲積碳。 舊款、結構簡單、負荷細嘅引擎。有啲車房仲會幫十幾年嘅車入呢種。只要係原廠設計啱、又勤力換，其實冇問題。 香港又熱又塞車，佢衰得最快。渦輪車千祈唔好用。
- 半合成 (中間落墨; 約 7,000–8,000 公里 或 6 個月): 礦物基礎油撈一部分合成基礎油。耐熱過礦物油，又平過全合成。 有返咁上下里數嘅舊車，或者想安全啲但唔想畀全合成價錢嘅車主。好多時配 10W-40。 「半合成」呢個叫法冇好嚴格規管，唔同牌子入面合成成分差好遠。
- 全合成 (多數新車必須用; 約 8,000–10,000 公里 或 12 個月): 分子大細做到好均勻嘅基礎油（Group III／PAO／酯類）。凍機流得快、高溫油膜頂得住、唔易結油泥，換油周期長好多。 凡係渦輪、缸內直噴、油電混能，或者大概近 10–15 年出嘅車。你本手冊如果寫住好長嘅換油周期，佢預咗你用全合成。 每公升貴啲，但周期長咗，計落多數打和。唔好淨係見支油寫住「Long Life」就自己拉長周期，跟手冊上限。

## 機油規格
標號（5W-30）只係答咗一半，另一半係規格 —— 支油樽上面印住嗰啲標準組織認證或者車廠認證。呢部分最多人跳過，但偏偏就係真正保護新引擎嗰部分。

### API（美國標準）
- API SP: 最新汽油標準（2020 年起）。加咗防 LSPI 保護 —— 一種低速預燃爆震，可以整死細排氣量渦輪直噴引擎。向下兼容，所以由 SN 升做 SP 一定安全。
- API SN / SN Plus: 上一代。舊款自然吸氣引擎仲用得。
- API CK-4 / CJ-4: 柴油引擎專用機油。

### ILSAC（慳油認證）
- GF-6A: 配 API SP，適用 0W-20／5W-30 或以上。有慳油同定時鏈磨蝕測試。
- GF-6B: 淨係畀好稀嘅 0W-16 用。

### ACEA（歐洲標準）
- A3/B4: 全 SAPS（高灰分）高強度歐洲油。冇微粒濾網嘅舊款歐洲汽油／柴油車。
- C2 / C3 / C5: 低 SAPS（低灰分）。如果你部車有柴油 DPF 或者汽油 GPF 微粒濾網，一定要用。灰分用錯會慢慢塞死個濾網，換一個好貴。

### 車廠認證
- VW 502.00 / 504.00 / 508.00: 福士、Audi、Škoda、SEAT。504.00 係長效低灰分嗰隻。
- MB 229.5 / 229.51 / 229.52: 平治。
- BMW LL-01 / LL-04: 寶馬同 MINI。有微粒濾網就用 LL-04。
- GM dexos1 Gen3: 多數近年 GM 汽油引擎。
- Porsche A40 / C30: 保時捷。
- 豐田／本田／日產原廠: 日系車廠多數淨係指定 API／ILSAC 加一個標號，所以日本車買油最易搞掂。
一句記住：先對標號，再對規格。兩樣都啱咗之後，好牌子之間嘅分別係有，但好細 —— 細過「準時換」同「拖到先換」之間嘅分別。

## 咁究竟幾時要換？
先睇車主手冊 —— 佢可能寫 5,000 公里，又可能寫 15,000 公里。跟住再按你實際點揸去調整。
- 喺香港揸車，基本上算「嚴苛使用」: 多數手冊都有第二個、短啲嘅「嚴苛條件」周期。香港嘅用車情況幾乎全中：短途行到引擎都未真正熱身、成日塞車停停行行、長期又熱又濕、斜路多、又成日怠速等人。如果你本手冊寫住兩個周期，就跟短嗰個。
- 每次換油都要一齊換機油隔: 一個隔嘅價錢只係機油嘅幾分之一。慳返個隔，即係留咗成隔舊油污喺個系統入面。
- 就算行得好少，一年都要換一次: 機油唔淨係「行得多」先變壞，佢會吸水氣同未燒完嘅汽油。一年只行三千公里嘅車，一樣要一年換一次。
- 記得一齊記低里數，唔好淨係記日期: 公里數同月數，邊個先到就以邊個為準。
- 「添油」唔等於「換油」: 加返啲油只係補返油位，補唔到已經消耗晒嘅添加劑。爭返啲時間啫。

### 呢啲情況即係拖得太耐
- 機油尺抹出嚟又黑又有粒粒，唔再係半透明嘅啡色
- 凍機著車嗰陣引擎聲大咗、或者「答答」聲多咗
- 機油警告燈著，或者油位跌到 MIN 或以下
- 打開車頭冚有陣燒燶味
- 你真係諗唔起上次幾時換 —— 咁其實已經係答案

### 點樣睇機油尺
- 泊喺平地。著車熱身，熄匙，等 5–10 分鐘畀機油流返落去。
- 抽出機油尺，用紙巾抹乾淨，再插返到底。
- 再抽出嚟睇。油位應該喺 MIN 同 MAX 之間 —— 貼近 MAX 冇問題，過咗 MAX 就唔得。
- 順便睇顏色。新油係蜜糖色、半透明；舊油就係唔透光嘅黑色。

## 常見誤解
- 換得越密越好？ → 過咗某個點就純粹嘥錢兼嘥資源。全合成每 3,000 公里換一次，係倒咗啲仲好好哋嘅油。跟手冊嘅「嚴苛條件」周期就夠，唔使再密。
- 全合成一定係升級？ → 對多數新引擎係啱，但一部好舊、里數好高、油封已經硬化嘅引擎，換咗之後可能會開始滲油 —— 因為之前係啲稠啲嘅礦物油頂住咗啲罅。跟規格，唔好跟廣告。
- 唔同牌子撈埋會出事？ → 只要標號同規格一樣，其實係兼容嘅 —— 半路急住添少少第二隻牌子，唔會搞出事。淨係唔好長期咁做啫，因為你會溝淡咗自己畀錢買嗰個添加劑配方。
- 每次換油都要「洗引擎」？ → 而家嘅機油本身有清潔添加劑，日常已經幫你保持乾淨。喺高里數引擎落化學清洗劑，隨時會沖甩啲本來塞住細滲漏嘅積碳。除非師傅真係發現有油泥問題，否則唔使做。
- 舊車用稠啲嘅油會保護好啲？ → 有時係，但唔可以當定律。冇原因就跳高一級，只會令凍機時流動慢咗 —— 而磨蝕就係嗰陣發生。想改就搵真係睇過你部引擎嘅師傅先。
- 睇顏色就知幾時要換？ → 油變黑好多時只係代表佢做緊嘢 —— 清潔添加劑將積碳懸浮住。顏色係好弱嘅指標，里數同時間先係真。有冇粒粒同有冇燶味，重要過深淺色。

## 油站服務
香港嘅油站幫到你咩、幫唔到你咩。每個站實際有咩服務都唔同 —— 下面呢張表係「可以留意咩」，唔係保證。你去過確認咗之後，記得喺我的油站入面存低附近嗰幾間。

### 通常都有
- 車胎氣泵（打氣）: 多數站都有，好多時係自助，免費或者好平。但唔係間間都有 —— 細地盤嘅油站有時會冇。開口問嘅話，員工通常都肯幫手。
- 便利店: 玻璃水、添加用嘅機油、雨刮、燈膽、水箱水、紙巾。急用好方便，但貴過汽車用品舖。
- 油卡／會員折扣: 幾乎每個牌子都有會員價或者信用卡回贈。第一日就去登記係值得嘅 —— 香港油價咁貴，每公升爭幾蚊，加埋好快見到。
- 洗手間: 多數有人手駐守嘅站都有。

### 部分站先有
- 洗車: 部分較大嘅站有自動洗車機。人手洗車多數係另一門生意。
- 電動車充電: 越嚟越多站有，但仲未算普及。真係要靠佢就記得開返營運商個 App 睇實時狀況。
- 快速換機油: 少數品牌站有服務區。但香港大部分換機油，仲係喺獨立車房或者代理度做。
- 車胎充氮氣: 呢個通常係車胎鋪嘅服務，多過油站。

### 呢啲要去第二度
- 換胎、補胎、車呔平衡、四輪定位: 車胎鋪。你喺度做嘢嗰陣，佢通常會順便免費幫你校返胎壓 —— 呢個係最慳事又最準嘅做法。
- 定期保養、換機油、迫力、電腦檢查: 獨立車房，或者品牌代理。代理貴啲但保住原廠保養；好嘅車房平啲，值得早啲搵定一間熟。
- 驗車: 指定汽車檢驗中心。私家車首次登記滿六年之後，每年續牌之前都要驗一次。
- 石油氣（LPG）: 專門嘅石油氣加氣站。主要係的士同小巴用，一般汽油私家車唔關事。

### 入 95 定 98？
- 香港油站賣 95 同 98 辛烷值無鉛汽油，另加柴油。個數字係辛烷值 —— 即係抗爆震能力，本身唔等於「馬力」或者「乾淨」。
- 跟手冊寫嘅最低要求就得。如果佢寫 95，你入 98 唔會令一部用唔到嘅引擎多咗馬力；你只係買咗用唔着嘅餘裕。
- 但如果佢寫 98 或者「須用高辛烷值汽油」，就唔好喺呢度慳 —— 渦輪引擎食低辛烷值會自動延遲點火，長期落去會傷。
- 汽油同柴油永遠唔可以互換。入錯油係新手最貴嘅意外之一 —— 未變成習慣之前，每次都望實個油槍顏色同標籤。

### 油站品牌
- Shell（蜆殼）: 全港網絡大。有 FuelSave 95 同 V-Power 98 無鉛汽油同柴油；好多站有便利店同氣泵。
- Caltex（加德士）: 汽油加 Techron 添加劑，另有柴油。Star Card／會員折扣好常見；唔少站有洗車。
- 中石化（Sinopec）: 95 同 98 無鉛汽油同柴油，會員價錢好多時比較進取。
- 中石油（PetroChina）: 95 同 98 無鉛汽油同柴油，網絡同優惠都相近。
- Esso（埃索）: 部分站提供 Synergy 汽油同柴油。

## 打氣一步一步教
全世界最平嘅保養，偏偏又係新車主最容易漏咗嗰樣。胎壓唔夠會耗油、兩邊胎肩磨蝕，亦係爆胎嘅頭號原因。
- 搵你部車嘅標準胎壓 —— 唔係條胎嘅: 貼紙喺駕駛席車門邊嘅門柱（打開門望門框），有時喺油缸蓋入面或者車主手冊。千祈唔好照跟胎側面印住嗰個數字 —— 嗰個係條胎嘅上限，唔係你部車嘅設定。一般私家車：32–36 psi。
- 要「凍胎」量度: 凍胎即係泊咗三個鐘以上，或者行咗少過約三公里。行過車啲氣熱脹，讀數會高 2–4 psi。如果一定要熱住量，就先打到目標值，之後凍返再覆核。
- 搞清楚單位: 1 bar ≈ 14.5 psi ≈ 100 kPa。香港啲氣泵多數用 psi。貼紙如果兩個單位都有，睇返你部氣泵用嗰個。
- 喺氣泵度點做: 扭開氣咀蓋，放喺唔會唔見嘅位。將氣喉頭直直㩒實個氣咀 —— 「嘶」一聲好正常，但一直漏氣就代表冇㩒正。加氣或者放氣，直到錶面到你個目標值。做完扭返個蓋。
- 四條胎都要 —— 連士啤呔: 士啤呔擺喺度冇人理，會慢慢漏氣，等到真係要用嗰陣多數已經冇氣。細嘅備用呔（space-saver）好多時要打好高，成 60 psi 左右 —— 睇返佢自己個標籤。
- 載重多就要加壓: 好多貼紙都會列多一組「滿載」或者長途高速用嘅較高胎壓。成車人成車行李嘅話，就跟嗰組。
- 需要嘅話 reset 胎壓感應: 如果部車有 TPMS 胎壓監測，打完氣可能要 reset 先熄到個警告燈 —— 做法喺手冊入面，多數係㩒個掣或者入 menu。

### 幾耐做一次
- 一個月一次，另外長途出發之前再check一次。
- 車胎自己每個月都會走大約 1–2 psi。
- 氣溫每跌 5–6°C，胎壓大約跌 1 psi，所以一凍親成個城市都會有人著胎壓燈。

### 胎壓唔啱會點
- 太低: 兩邊外側磨蝕、扭軚重、耗油、條胎行到好熱 —— 呢個就係典型爆胎前奏。
- 太高: 中間一條磨蝕、坐落好彈、落雨抓地差咗（因為接地面積細咗）。
- 淨係一邊磨蝕: 呢個唔關胎壓事，係四輪定位問題。搵人check下。

### 順便睇埋呢啲
- 花紋深度：香港法例最低係 1.6 毫米。多數車胎喺坑紋入面有磨蝕指示條，花紋磨到同條指示條平就代表條胎壽終正寢。
- 睇下有冇割痕、胎側有冇隆起、有冇釘。胎側隆起就即刻換，唔好等。
- 睇埋出廠年份：胎側有個四位數 DOT 碼，代表週數同年份（例如 2523＝2023 年第 25 週）。橡膠會隨年份變硬 —— 多數廠商建議就算仲有花紋，五至六年都應該換，而香港啲熱同紫外線更加加速。

## 香港手續
- 車輛牌照（續牌／牌費）: 過期之前要續 —— 冇有效牌照上路係違法嘅。續牌期通常係 4 個月或者 12 個月；運輸署會寄提示，但唔好淨係靠封信。
- 第三者保險: 法例規定任何時候都要有效。續牌之前保險一定要生效。
- 驗車: 私家車由首次登記計滿六年之後，每次續牌前都要去指定檢驗中心年檢。早啲 book —— 位好搶。
- 文件放車上: 車輛登記文件資料、保險證同牌照。順便影低相擺喺手機做後備。

# REFERENCE (English)

## Accident
Nobody plans for this, so the first minutes are always chaotic. Work down the list in order. Everything here is general guidance for Hong Kong — the police, your insurer and the Transport Department are the authorities.

### Right now: stop and make it safe
- Stop. Do not drive off: Failing to stop after an accident is a criminal offence in Hong Kong, even if it looks minor and even if you think it was not your fault.
- Hazard lights on: Then, if it is safe, put a warning triangle well behind the car — much further back on an expressway than on a street.
- Get people out of danger: On an expressway, get everyone out of the car and behind the barrier. A stopped car in a live lane is the most dangerous place to be.
- Anyone hurt? Call 999: Do not move an injured person unless they are in immediate danger (fire, traffic). Say where you are, how many people, and what you can see.

### The first minute after a hard impact
- Check yourself before you move: Adrenaline hides injury. Wiggle fingers and toes, check for neck pain, look for blood. If your neck or back hurts, stay still and wait for the ambulance unless the car is on fire.
- Airbag smoke is normal: The white powder and burning smell after an airbag fires is the propellant, not a fire. Dark smoke, flames, or a strong petrol smell is different — get everyone out and at least 30 m away, and do not open the bonnet.
- Engine off, hazards on: Turn the key off if you can reach it. It cuts the fuel pump and reduces fire risk.
- Trapped? Do not force it: Call 999, say you are trapped, and keep talking to them. Do not let bystanders pull you out unless there is fire — being dragged out with a spinal injury does more harm than waiting.
- Children in car seats: Leave a child buckled in unless there is immediate danger. If they complain of neck or back pain, or are drowsy, wait for paramedics to move them.
- Then the basics: Once everyone is stable: get out on the side away from traffic, behind a barrier, triangle out. Now the general steps below apply.

### Someone is hurt
- Call 999 first, then help: Say the location, how many injured, whether anyone is unconscious or trapped. Put the phone on speaker so your hands are free.
- Do not move anyone with a possible neck or back injury: Unless fire or traffic makes staying more dangerous. Support the head still if you can.
- Bleeding: press hard and keep pressing: A clean cloth, a T-shirt, anything. Do not lift it to look. Elevate the limb if you can.
- Unconscious but breathing: If you have first-aid training, recovery position. If not, keep the airway clear and wait. Stay with them.
- Keep them warm and talking: Shock sets in fast. A jacket, a calm voice. Do not give food or water.
- Do not leave: Even if you feel fine and the other party is being difficult. Stay until police release you — leaving an injured person is a serious offence.

### The other driver drove off
- Do not chase: Get the plate, colour, make, direction, and the time. A partial plate still helps. Shout it into your phone's voice memo if you cannot write.
- Save the dashcam clip now: Most dashcams overwrite in a loop within hours. Pull the card or lock the file before you do anything else.
- Witnesses and cameras: Ask anyone nearby for a name and number. Note shops, buildings and buses that may have cameras — the police can request footage, but only if they know where to ask.
- Report immediately: Call 999 if anyone is hurt; otherwise the police report hotline or a station, within 24 hours. You need the report number for any claim.
- If they are never found: Comprehensive cover pays for your car. For injuries caused by an untraced or uninsured driver, the Motor Insurers' Bureau of Hong Kong (汽車保險局) runs a compensation scheme — ask your insurer or a lawyer about it.

### The days after a serious crash
- Get checked even if you feel fine: Whiplash, concussion and internal bruising often appear 24–72 hours later. See A&E or a GP the same day, say it was a car crash, and keep every receipt and report — an injury claim is impossible without a medical record from the start.
- Do not drive shaken: Adrenaline wears off suddenly. Get someone to collect you, or take a taxi. It is not weakness, it is the second accident you are avoiding.
- Statements: Give the police a factual statement. Do not give a recorded statement to the other side's insurer or adjuster until you have spoken to your own insurer — you are not obliged to.
- If the car is written off: The insurer offers the market value, not what you paid. You can dispute with evidence (recent valuation, service history, low mileage). Any outstanding car loan is still due — check whether your policy has gap cover.
- Injury claims: Third-party bodily injury is claimed against the at-fault driver's insurer. Keep a simple diary: pain, missed work, expenses, receipts. Personal-injury claims in Hong Kong generally have a limitation period of years, not months — but evidence does not keep, so start early.
- If you get a summons: Careless-driving charges follow many serious crashes, even when it felt like nobody's fault. Do not plead by post without advice. The Duty Lawyer Service runs a free legal advice scheme; your insurer may also provide a lawyer.
- Look after your head: It is normal to replay it, to be scared of the road, to not sleep. If it is still like that after a few weeks, that is worth talking to a doctor about too.

### Do I have to call the police?
- Yes — if anyone is injured: Always. The police must attend and a report number (RN) is issued. You will need that number for the insurance claim.
- Yes — if the other party leaves, is uncooperative, or you hit an unattended car: If you cannot exchange particulars at the scene, you must report to a police station within 24 hours. Hitting a parked car and driving off is treated as failing to stop.
- Yes — if a government vehicle, taxi, bus or minibus is involved: These are almost always reported. Let the police handle it.
- Optional — minor damage, nobody hurt, both drivers cooperative: You may exchange particulars and leave. Even then, many insurers prefer a police report — check your policy. When in doubt, report.

### What to exchange with the other driver
- Vehicle registration mark (plate number)
- Driver's full name and HKID / driving licence number
- Contact phone number
- Insurance company and policy number
- Registered owner's name, if it is not the driver

### Photograph before anything moves
- Both cars exactly where they stopped, from several angles and from a distance showing the road layout
- Every point of damage on both cars, close up
- Both number plates, clearly readable
- The road: lane markings, signs, traffic lights, skid marks, road surface, weather
- The other driver's licence and insurance card, if they let you
- Any witnesses — get a name and phone number before they leave

### Do not
- Do not admit fault: Not to the other driver, not on the phone, not on social media. Describe what happened factually. Fault is decided later, by the insurers or a court.
- Do not settle in cash at the roadside: A private deal can void your insurance cover, and hidden damage is often discovered later. Let the insurers handle it.
- Do not sign anything from the other party: Except an official police form.
- Do not leave with an injured person still at the scene: Stay until the police release you.

### In the next 24–48 hours
- Notify your insurer: Most policies require notice within 24–48 hours or 'as soon as practicable'. Call the hotline on your insurance card and give the police RN if you have one.
- Use the insurer's towing: If the car cannot be driven, call the insurer's 24-hour towing line rather than any tow truck that turns up. Unsolicited tows at accident scenes are a known problem.
- Choose the repair route: Third-party-only cover: you claim against the other driver's insurer (or pay yourself). Comprehensive: your own insurer repairs it and recovers from the other side. Ask before authorising any repair.
- Keep everything: Photos, the RN, the other party's details, receipts, the tow invoice. Put a note in this app's records too so you have the date and mileage.

### Not a crash — the car just died
- Hazard lights, pull as far left as you safely can — onto the hard shoulder on an expressway.
- On an expressway: everyone out and behind the barrier, triangle well back. Do not try to fix anything in a live lane.
- Call your insurer's roadside/towing line. Many comprehensive policies include it.
- A flat tyre on a busy road is often safer towed than changed on the spot.

### Keep these in the car
- Warning triangle and a reflective vest
- Torch (phone torch counts, but a real one lasts longer)
- A copy of the insurance card and this app on your phone
- Basic first-aid kit and a phone charger cable
- Pen and paper — phones die at the worst moment

### Numbers
- 999: Police / ambulance / fire — any emergency
- Your insurer's hotline: On your insurance card. Save it in your phone now.
- 1823: Government hotline, including Transport Department enquiries

## Traffic rules
The rules a new driver actually gets caught by. This is a reminder, not the law — the Transport Department's Road Users' Code is the authority, and the numbers below change from time to time.

### The basics
- Drive on the left: Overtake on the right. On multi-lane roads, keep left unless overtaking.
- 50 km/h unless a sign says otherwise: That is the default limit on every Hong Kong road without a posted limit. Expressways post 70, 80, 100 or 110.
- Seatbelts for everyone: Front and rear, every seat, every trip. The driver is responsible for passengers.
- No hand-held phone: Hands-free mounted use only. Holding the phone at a red light still counts.
- Alcohol limit: 50 mg per 100 ml of blood: That is roughly one drink for most people — and random breath tests are common. The safe number is zero.
- Headlights on from sunset to sunrise: And in tunnels, heavy rain, or poor visibility. Many cars do not switch on automatically.

### Junctions and lines
- Yellow box: Do not enter unless your exit is clear. The only exception is waiting to turn right when only oncoming traffic is stopping you.
- Zebra crossing: Pedestrians have priority the moment they step on. Slow down on approach; never overtake near one.
- Amber light means stop: Unless stopping would be unsafe. It does not mean 'speed up'.
- Roundabouts: Give way to traffic already on the roundabout, coming from your right. Signal left before your exit.
- Solid white lines: No crossing or changing lane — including inside tunnels, where the lines are solid for the whole length.
- Bus lanes: Off limits during the hours on the sign. Cameras enforce these.

### Stopping and parking
- Double yellow line: No parking at any time. Single yellow: no parking during the hours on the sign.
- Never stop within a bus stop, on a crossing, or near a junction: Stopping 'just for a minute' at a bus stop is one of the most ticketed offences in the city.
- Metered parking: Pay for the full time you expect to stay. Overstaying is a fixed penalty.
- Leaving the car running: Idling an engine while parked is an offence with fixed exceptions. Switch off.

### Probationary (P-plate) drivers — first 12 months
- Display P plates: Front and rear, whenever you drive.
- Max 70 km/h: Regardless of the posted limit.
- No right-most lane on expressways with three or more lanes: The offside lane is off limits to you.
- Fewer points before you lose the licence: Probationary drivers are disqualified with fewer demerit points than full-licence holders, and the probation period may be extended. Be careful for a year.

### Demerit points (DOPS)
- 15 points in 2 years = disqualified: Points stay on your record for two years from the date of the offence.
- Speeding: More than 15 km/h over: 3 points. More than 30 over: 5. More than 45 over: 10 — plus a court summons at the top end.
- Running a red light: 5 points.
- Careless driving: 5 points. Dangerous driving: 10 points, and usually a court appearance.
- Not obeying a traffic sign or line: 3 points.
- Phone, seatbelt, parking: Fixed-penalty fines. The exact amounts and whether points attach change — check the Transport Department's current schedule.

### Habits that keep a new driver out of trouble
- Two-second gap: Pick a fixed object; when the car ahead passes it, count 'one-thousand-one, one-thousand-two'. If you pass it before you finish, you are too close. Double it in rain.
- Mirrors, signal, blind spot, move: Every lane change, every time. Hong Kong minibuses and motorcycles live in blind spots.
- Assume every taxi and minibus will stop without warning: Because it will.
- Slow before the corner, not in it: Especially on the wet, painted road markings — they are slippery.
- Handbrake on every hill start: Hong Kong is steep. Learn hill starts in an empty car park, not at Garden Road.
The Road Users' Code (道路使用者守則) is published free by the Transport Department and is the definitive reference. Keep the PDF on your phone.

## Engine oil: how to choose
- Open the manual (or the filler cap): Find two things: the viscosity grade (e.g. 5W-30) and the specification (e.g. API SP, ACEA C3, VW 504.00). Write both into My Car.
- Lock the grade: Non-negotiable. Do not let anyone talk you into a different grade without a specific reason for your engine.
- Match or exceed the spec: API standards are backwards compatible — SP covers a car asking for SN. Carmaker approvals are not flexible: if it says VW 504.00, it needs VW 504.00.
- Pick the oil type: If the manual asks for a long interval or the engine is turbocharged, that means full synthetic. Otherwise semi-synthetic is a fair compromise on an older car.
- Then pick a brand: Now it is about price, availability and preference. Any of the majors that carries your grade + spec will do the job.
- Ask for the old parts back: A quick way to keep a garage honest: ask to see the drained oil and the old filter. Log the date, mileage, brand and grade in CarMate straight away.

## Reading the grade: what 5W-30 means
5W — the "W" is Winter. It rates how easily the oil still flows when the engine is cold. Lower number = thinner when cold = it reaches the top of the engine faster on start-up, which is when most engine wear happens.
30 — how thick the oil stays at full operating temperature (100°C). Higher number = thicker film.
In Hong Kong there is no real winter, so the W number matters less here than in a cold country. That is not a licence to change it: modern engines have very tight oil passages and variable valve timing that is designed around one grade. Too thick and oil arrives late; too thin and the film breaks down. Use what the manual says.
- 0W-16 / 0W-20: Modern Japanese engines, hybrids, small turbos. Fuel-economy grades — only use if specified.
- 5W-30: The most common grade worldwide. Most modern Japanese and European petrol cars.
- 5W-40: European turbo petrol and diesel, older performance engines, hot hard driving.
- 10W-40: Older or higher-mileage engines, usually semi-synthetic. Common on cars 10+ years old.
- 15W-40: Older diesels and commercial vans. Rarely right for a modern private car.
- 0W-40: High-performance European engines (Porsche A40, some AMG). Premium full synthetic only.
Where to find your grade: the owner's manual, the oil filler cap on top of the engine, or a sticker under the bonnet. Put it in My Car so you never have to look it up at the garage again.

## Oil types
- Mineral oil (Cheapest; ≈5,000 km or 3–4 months): Straight refined crude oil. The molecules are uneven sizes, so it thins out faster under heat and leaves more deposits. Older, simple, low-stress engines. Some workshops still put it in 15-year-old cars. Fine if it is what the car was designed for and you change it often. Degrades fastest in Hong Kong's heat and stop-go traffic. Never use it in a turbo engine.
- Semi-synthetic (Middle ground; ≈7,000–8,000 km or 6 months): A blend of mineral base oil with some synthetic base. Better heat resistance than mineral, cheaper than full synthetic. Older cars with some mileage on them, or drivers who want a safety margin without paying full-synthetic prices. Often paired with 10W-40. "Semi-synthetic" is loosely regulated — the synthetic share varies a lot between brands.
- Full synthetic (Most modern cars require this; ≈8,000–10,000 km or 12 months): Base oil built to a uniform molecular size (Group III / PAO / ester). Flows better cold, holds its film hotter, resists sludge, and lasts far longer between changes. Anything turbocharged, direct-injection, hybrid, or made in roughly the last 10–15 years. If your manual asks for a long service interval, it is assuming full synthetic. Costs more per litre but the longer interval usually evens it out. Do not stretch the interval past what your manual allows just because the bottle says "long life".

## Oil specifications
The grade (5W-30) is only half the answer. The other half is the specification — the standards body or carmaker approval printed on the bottle. This is the part people skip, and it is the part that actually protects a modern engine.

### API (American)
- API SP: Newest petrol standard (2020+). Adds protection against LSPI — a pre-ignition knock that can wreck small turbo direct-injection engines. Backwards compatible, so SP is a safe upgrade over SN.
- API SN / SN Plus: Previous generation. Still fine for older non-turbo engines.
- API CK-4 / CJ-4: Diesel engine oils.

### ILSAC (fuel economy)
- GF-6A: Pairs with API SP for 0W-20 / 5W-30 and thicker. Fuel-economy and timing-chain wear tested.
- GF-6B: For the very thin 0W-16 grade only.

### ACEA (European)
- A3/B4: Full-SAPS, high-strength European oils. Older Euro petrol and diesel without a particulate filter.
- C2 / C3 / C5: Low-SAPS ('mid' ash). Required if your car has a diesel DPF or a petrol GPF filter. Wrong ash level slowly clogs the filter — an expensive mistake.

### Carmaker approvals
- VW 502.00 / 504.00 / 508.00: Volkswagen, Audi, Škoda, SEAT. 504.00 is the long-life low-SAPS one.
- MB 229.5 / 229.51 / 229.52: Mercedes-Benz.
- BMW LL-01 / LL-04: BMW and MINI. LL-04 for particulate-filter cars.
- GM dexos1 Gen3: Most modern GM petrol engines.
- Porsche A40 / C30: Porsche.
- Toyota / Honda / Nissan genuine: Japanese makers mostly just specify API/ILSAC plus a grade, which is why Japanese cars are easy to shop for.
Rule of thumb: match the grade, then match the spec. Once both match, the difference between good brands is real but small — much smaller than the difference between changing on time and changing late.

## When should I actually change it?
Your manual comes first — it may say anything from 5,000 km to 15,000 km. Then adjust for how the car is really used.
- Hong Kong counts as "severe service": Most manuals have a second, shorter interval for severe conditions. Hong Kong driving ticks nearly every box on that list: short trips that never fully warm the engine, heavy stop-go traffic, sustained heat and humidity, steep hills, and long idling. If your manual gives two intervals, use the shorter one.
- Change the oil filter every single time: A filter costs a fraction of the oil. Reusing it leaves a filter full of old dirty oil in the system.
- Even a low-mileage car needs an annual change: Oil ages by absorbing moisture and unburnt fuel, not just by covering distance. A car that does 3,000 km a year still needs fresh oil once a year.
- Log the mileage, not just the date: Whichever limit arrives first — km or months — is the one that counts.
- Topping up is not changing: Adding a bit of oil restores the level, not the additives. It buys you time, nothing more.

### Signs it is overdue
- Dipstick oil is black and gritty rather than translucent brown
- Engine sounds louder or more 'tappy' on a cold start
- Oil warning light, or the level is at or below MIN
- Burnt smell from the engine bay
- You genuinely cannot remember the last change — that is itself the answer

### How to check the dipstick
- Park on level ground. Run the engine to warm it, switch off, wait 5–10 minutes for the oil to drain back down.
- Pull the dipstick out, wipe it clean with tissue, push it fully back in.
- Pull it out again and read it. The level should sit between MIN and MAX — closer to MAX is fine, above MAX is not.
- Look at the colour too. Fresh oil is honey-coloured and translucent; old oil is opaque black.

## Myths
- More frequent changes are always better → Past a point it is just money and waste. Changing full synthetic every 3,000 km throws away oil that is still fine. Follow the manual's severe-service interval and stop there.
- Full synthetic is always the right upgrade → It is right for most modern engines, but a very old, high-mileage engine with worn seals can start weeping through gaps that thicker mineral oil was bridging. Match the spec, not the marketing.
- You must never mix brands → All oils of the same grade and spec are compatible — an emergency top-up with a different brand will not hurt anything. It is just not ideal as a permanent habit, since you dilute whichever additive package you paid for.
- Engine flush before every change → Modern detergent oils keep the engine clean on their own. A chemical flush on a high-mileage engine can dislodge deposits that were plugging small leaks. Only do it if a mechanic finds an actual sludge problem.
- Thicker oil protects an old engine better → Sometimes, but not by default. Going up a grade without a reason slows cold-start flow, which is when wear happens. Ask a mechanic who has actually looked at your engine.
- The colour tells you when to change → Dark oil is often just oil doing its job — detergents hold soot in suspension. Colour is a weak signal; mileage and time are the real ones. Grittiness and smell matter more than shade.

## Petrol station services
What a Hong Kong petrol station can and cannot do for you. Which services a site actually has varies station by station — the list below is what to look for, not a guarantee. Save the ones near you in My stations once you have checked.

### Usually available
- Air pump for tyres (打氣): Most sites have one, often self-service and free or very cheap. Not every station — small forecourts sometimes skip it. Attendants will usually help if you ask.
- Convenience shop: Screen wash, top-up engine oil, wiper blades, bulbs, coolant, tissue. Handy for emergencies, dearer than an auto parts shop.
- Fuel-card / loyalty discount: Almost all brands run member pricing or card rebates. Worth signing up on day one — HK fuel is expensive enough that a few dollars a litre adds up fast.
- Toilet: Most staffed sites.

### Some stations only
- Car wash: Automatic rollover wash at selected larger sites. Hand wash is usually a separate business.
- EV charging: Appearing at more sites, but still patchy. Check the operator's app before relying on it.
- Quick oil change bay: A few branded sites run a service bay. Most oil changes in Hong Kong still happen at an independent garage (車房) or the dealer.
- Nitrogen tyre fill: More typically a tyre shop service than a petrol station one.

### Go elsewhere for these
- Tyre replacement, puncture repair, balancing, alignment: Tyre shop (車胎鋪). They will normally check and set your pressures for free while you are there — the easiest way to get it done properly.
- Servicing, oil change, brakes, diagnostics: Independent garage (車房) or the franchised dealer (代理). Dealer costs more and protects the warranty; a good garage costs less and is worth finding early.
- Roadworthiness test (驗車): A designated vehicle examination centre — required annually once a private car is over six years old, before you can renew the licence.
- LPG (石油氣): Dedicated LPG filling stations. Relevant to taxis and light buses, not to a normal petrol private car.

### 95 or 98?
- Hong Kong pumps sell 95 and 98 octane unleaded plus diesel. The number is the octane rating — resistance to knocking, not 'power' or 'cleanliness' by itself.
- Use the minimum your manual specifies. If it says 95, running 98 will not add power to an engine that cannot use it; you are paying for headroom you do not need.
- If it says 98 or 'premium unleaded required', do not save money there — a knock-limited turbo engine will pull timing on lower octane and can suffer over time.
- Diesel and petrol are never interchangeable. Misfuelling is one of the most expensive mistakes a new owner can make — check the pump nozzle colour and label every single time until it is automatic.

### Station brands
- Shell: Large network across the territory. FuelSave 95 and V-Power 98 unleaded plus diesel; many sites have a convenience shop and an air pump.
- Caltex: Petrol with Techron additive plus diesel. Star Card / loyalty discounts are common; many sites have a car wash.
- Sinopec 中石化: 95 and 98 unleaded plus diesel, often with aggressive member pricing.
- PetroChina 中石油: 95 and 98 unleaded plus diesel, similar footprint and promotions.
- Esso: Synergy petrol and diesel at selected sites.

## Filling your tyres, step by step
The single cheapest piece of maintenance there is, and the one new owners skip most. Under-inflated tyres cost you fuel, wear out at the shoulders, and are the main cause of blowouts.
- Find your correct pressure — not the tyre's: It is on a sticker in the driver's door jamb (open the door and look at the frame), sometimes inside the fuel flap or in the manual. Do not use the number moulded on the tyre sidewall — that is the tyre's maximum, not your car's setting. Typical private car: 32–36 psi.
- Check cold: Cold means parked 3+ hours, or driven less than about 3 km. Driving heats the air and reads 2–4 psi high. If you must check warm, set it to the target and re-check cold later.
- Know your units: 1 bar ≈ 14.5 psi ≈ 100 kPa. Hong Kong pumps are usually psi. If a placard shows both, read the one your pump uses.
- At the pump: Unscrew the valve cap and keep it somewhere you will not lose it. Press the hose fitting squarely onto the valve — a short hiss is normal, a continuous hiss means it is not seated. Add or release air until the gauge reads your target. Screw the cap back on.
- Do all four — and the spare: Spare tyres lose pressure sitting untouched and are almost always flat when you finally need one. Space-saver spares often need a much higher pressure (around 60 psi) — check its own label.
- Adjust for a full load: Many placards list a second, higher set of pressures for a fully loaded car or long motorway runs. Use those if you are packing the car.
- Reset the TPMS if needed: If your car has tyre-pressure monitoring, the warning light may need a reset after inflating — the procedure is in the manual, often a button or a menu item.

### How often
- Once a month, and before any long drive.
- Tyres lose roughly 1–2 psi a month on their own.
- Pressure drops about 1 psi for every 5–6°C the temperature falls, so a cold snap will set off warning lights across the city.

### What wrong pressure looks like
- Too low: Worn at both outer edges, heavy steering, worse fuel economy, tyre runs hot — the classic blowout setup.
- Too high: Worn down the centre strip, bouncy ride, less grip in the wet because the contact patch shrinks.
- Uneven wear on one side only: Not a pressure problem — that is alignment. Get it checked.

### While you are down there
- Tread depth: the legal minimum in Hong Kong is 1.6 mm. Most tyres have wear indicator bars moulded into the grooves — when the tread is flush with the bar, the tyre is finished.
- Look for cuts, bulges in the sidewall, or a nail head. A sidewall bulge means replace it now, not later.
- Check the age: a four-digit DOT code on the sidewall gives week and year (e.g. 2523 = week 25 of 2023). Rubber hardens with age — most makers say replace at 5–6 years even with tread left, and Hong Kong's heat and UV do not help.

## Hong Kong paperwork
- Vehicle licence (牌費): Renew before it expires — driving with an expired licence is an offence. Renewal periods are typically 4 or 12 months; the Transport Department sends a reminder but do not depend on the post.
- Third-party insurance: Legally required at all times. It must be valid before the licence can be renewed.
- Roadworthiness test (驗車): Once a private car is more than six years old from first registration, it needs an annual examination at a designated centre before each licence renewal. Book early — slots get tight.
- Keep the paperwork in the car: Registration document details, insurance certificate and licence. Photograph them and keep the photos on your phone as a backup.

## General service intervals
